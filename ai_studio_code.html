Essential Statistics Concepts

Review standard definitions, student-friendly formulas, and step-by-step examples before testing your skills.

1. Mean (The Arithmetic Average)

The mean is the balance point of a dataset. It is computed by summing every value and dividing by the total count.

$$\bar{x} = \frac{\sum x}{n} = \frac{x_1 + x_2 + \dots + x_n}{n}$$
Worked Example: Find the mean of 6, 8, 10, 16.
Sum = 6 + 8 + 10 + 16 = 40. Count = 4.
Mean = 40 / 4 = 10.

2. Median (The Middle Value)

The median is the exact center of sorted data. Unlike the mean, the median is resistant to extreme outliers.

Position = (n + 1) / 2  |  If n is even: Average of two center values
Worked Example: Find the median of 15, 3, 9, 21, 8.
1. Sort ascending: 3, 8, 9, 15, 21.
2. The middle (3rd) number is 9.

3. Mode (Most Frequent)

The mode is the observation that appears most often. A dataset can have one mode (unimodal), more than one mode (bimodal/multimodal), or no mode at all.

Worked Example: In {4, 7, 7, 9, 10, 7, 2}, 7 is the mode because it appears 3 times.

4. Range & Interquartile Range (IQR)

Measures of spread describe how far apart numbers are scattered.

Range = Maximum - Minimum  |  IQR = Q3 - Q1
Worked Example: For {2, 5, 8, 12, 19}:
Range = 19 - 2 = 17.

5. Frequency Tables & Grouped Mean

When data is grouped with frequencies $f$ and midpoints $m$, the mean is estimated using weighted totals:

Grouped Mean = $\frac{\sum (f \cdot m)}{\sum f}$
Worked Example: Score 5 (f = 2), Score 10 (f = 3).
Sum of (f × m) = (2 × 5) + (3 × 10) = 10 + 30 = 40.
Total f = 5. Estimated Mean = 40 / 5 = 8.

6. Variance & Standard Deviation

Variance measures the average squared distance from the mean. Standard deviation brings this value back into the original units of measurement.

Sample Variance: $s^2 = \frac{\sum (x - \bar{x})^2}{n - 1}$  |  Standard Deviation: $s = \sqrt{s^2}$
Sample vs. Population: Always use n - 1 in the denominator when calculating sample variance (Bessel's correction) to avoid underestimating true population variability.
